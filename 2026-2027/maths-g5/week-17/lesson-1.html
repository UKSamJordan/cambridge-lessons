Spot the Mistake

Linh finds the area of a right-angled triangle (base = 8 cm, height = 3 cm) and writes: "8 × 3 = 24 cm²". What did Linh forget?

Linh found the whole rectangle (24 cm²) and forgot to divide by 2! Correct area = (8 × 3) ÷ 2 = 12 cm².
Always, Sometimes, Never

"The area of a right-angled triangle with whole-number sides is an even whole number."

SOMETIMES TRUE! 4 × 3 ÷ 2 = 6 (even), 5 × 2 ÷ 2 = 5 (odd), and 5 × 3 ÷ 2 = 7.5 (a decimal!).
Convince Me

Nam says: "If I double the height of a right-angled triangle and keep the base the same, the area doubles." Prove it!

TRUE! Try b = 4 cm, h = 2 cm ➔ Area = 4 cm². Double h to 4 cm ➔ Area = (4 × 4) ÷ 2 = 8 cm² (exact double!).

Grid Square Counting

A right-angled triangle on a cm² grid covers 3 full squares and 6 half-squares. What is its area?

3 + (6 ÷ 2) = 3 + 3 = 6 cm²
🟠 Check 2 · Should

Half-Rectangle Formula

Calculate the exact area of a right-angled triangle with base = 8 cm and height = 9 cm.

(8 × 9) ÷ 2 = 72 ÷ 2 = 36 cm²

Reverse Enclosing Rectangle

A right-angled triangle has an area of 23 m². What is the area of the rectangle made by two copies?

23 × 2 = 46 m²

⚠️ Top 3 Misconceptions
  • Forgetting ÷ 2: Pupils find b × h (the rectangle) and stop. Remind: "Slice it in half!"
  • Sloping-Side Trap: Multiplying by the longest slanted side instead of the two perpendicular sides meeting the 90° box.
  • Wrong Units: Writing cm instead of cm² (or m instead of m²).

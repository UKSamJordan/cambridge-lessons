Example A · Halve the Even Side!

Full Rectangle: 6.5 × 4 = 26 cm²

Triangle Area = 26 ÷ 2 = 13 cm²

Smart Mental Trick: Halve 4 first (4 ÷ 2 = 2), then 6.5 × 2 = 13 cm²!

Example B · Decimal Height

Full Rectangle: 8 × 4.5 = 36 cm²

Triangle Area = 36 ÷ 2 = 18 cm²

Smart Mental Trick: Halve 8 first (8 ÷ 2 = 4), then 4 × 4.5 = 18 cm²!

Golden Rule: Rebuild the Rectangle First!

Problem

A right-angled triangle has Area = 24 cm² and Base = 8 cm. Find its height.

Step 1 · Double to Rebuild the Rectangle

24 × 2 = 48 cm²

Step 2 · Divide by Known Base

48 ÷ 8 = 6 cm

Check: 8 × 6 ÷ 2 = 24 cm² ✓

⚠️ Watch Out! Never do 24 ÷ 8 = 3 cm — that forgets the triangle was halved!

Linh vs Nam

A compound sign is a 8 cm × 5 cm rectangle with a right-angled triangle (base 4 cm, height 5 cm) joined to its side.

Linh: "Total area = 8 × 5 + 4 × 5 = 60 cm²."
Nam: "Total area = 8 × 5 + (4 × 5 ÷ 2) = 50 cm²."
✅ Nam is correct! Linh forgot to halve the triangle. Rectangle = 40 cm²; Triangle = 4 × 5 ÷ 2 = 10 cm². Total = 50 cm².
⚖️ Always · Sometimes · Never

Scaling a Triangle

"Doubling the base of a right-angled triangle while keeping the height fixed doubles its area."

Test with numbers on your whiteboard first!

✅ ALWAYS TRUE! Example: b = 4, h = 5 → Area = 10 cm². Double base to b = 8, h = 5 → Area = 20 cm² (exact double!).
💡 Convince Me!

Same Area, Different Shapes

Can two different compound shapes (each made from 1 rectangle + 1 right-angled triangle) both have total area 44 cm²?

✅ YES!
Shape 1: Rect 8×4 (32) + Tri 6×4÷2 (12) = 44 cm².
Shape 2: Rect 7×5 (35) + Tri 6×3÷2 (9) = 44 cm².

🚨 Top Misconception Alert

When finding a missing side from triangle area (e.g. Area = 24 cm², base = 8 cm), pupils divide directly (24 ÷ 8 = 3 cm). Always chant: "Rebuild the rectangle first — DOUBLE, then DIVIDE!"

🎯 Hinge Question (After Model 3)

"A right-angled triangle has Area = 15 cm² and base = 5 cm. Show on mini-whiteboards: A) 3 cm, B) 6 cm, C) 10 cm." If >20% show A (3 cm), snap two identical paper triangles together live on the visualiser.

💡 Decimal Strategy Tip

For 6.5 × 4 ÷ 2 or 8 × 4.5 ÷ 2, encourage pupils to halve the even whole number first (6.5 × 2 = 13; 4 × 4.5 = 18) to avoid messy decimal division.

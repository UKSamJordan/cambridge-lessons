Why Does This Matter in Real Life?

Where do real people see decimal hours every single day?

✈️

Flight Trackers & Pilots

Booking apps at Noi Bai Airport show flight times like 1.5 h or 2.4 h. Travellers convert them to hours and minutes to plan airport pick-ups!

⏱️

Sports Stopwatches & Apps

Cycling and running apps display total training time as 1.25 h or 0.75 h so coaches can calculate average speed easily.

💼

Work & Tutor Timesheets

Businesses pay staff by decimal hours (e.g. 7.5 h a day). Knowing 0.5 h = 30 min stops costly payroll mistakes!

Thinking and Working Mathematically (TWM)

🧠 Reasoning & Justification Lab

Don't just say True or False — prove it using base-60 reasoning!

Part A — True or False? (Tap each card to reveal the proof)

Part B1 · Who Is Correct?

A school film lasts 1.3 hours.

👧 Linh says:

"1.3 hours is 1 hour 30 minutes because .3 means 30."

👦 Nam says:

"1.3 hours is 1 hour 18 minutes because 0.1 h = 6 min and 3 × 6 = 18."

✅ Nam is correct! Linh used base-10 (100 minutes in an hour), but time uses base-60. Since 0.1 h = 6 min, 0.3 h = 3 × 6 = 18 minutes. So 1.3 h = 1 hour 18 minutes.
Part B2 · Always, Sometimes, or Never?

"When you convert any 1-decimal-place hour into hours and minutes, the number of minutes is a multiple of 6."

Test 3 examples (such as 0.1 h, 0.7 h, 2.4 h) before deciding!

✅ ALWAYS TRUE! Every tenth of an hour is 6 minutes (60 ÷ 10 = 6). Multiplying any whole number of tenths (1 to 9) by 6 always produces a multiple of 6: 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36, 42, 48, 54 minutes!

💬 Reasoning Sentence Stems (Speak Like a Mathematician)

"I disagree with ___ because time uses base-60, not base-10..."
"Since 0.1 hour equals 6 minutes, ___ tenths equals ___ × 6 = ___ minutes."
"I kept the whole hours (___ h) and multiplied only the decimal part (___ × 60)."

Lesson 1 Coaching Drawer

🚨 Red-Flag Misconceptions

  • Writing 0.5 h = 50 min or 1.3 h = 1 h 30 min (confusing base-10 with base-60).
  • Multiplying the whole hours by 60 as well as the decimal part.
  • Writing 5.1 h = 5 h 1 min instead of 5 h 6 min.

🪑 Teacher Support Table (4–6 Pupils)

Use a paper clock marked in 6-minute jumps (0.1 = 6, 0.2 = 12, 0.3 = 18 ... 0.9 = 54) and a Two-Box Split Mat: Box 1 keeps whole hours untouched; Box 2 multiplies only the decimal tenths by 6.

Why Are We Learning Decimals, Sequences & Negative Numbers?

🌡️ 🏢

Weather & Lifts

Mountain peaks like Fansipan drop below 0°C in winter, and Hanoi apartment lifts travel down to basement floors B1 (−1) and B2 (−2). Negative integers measure everything below zero!

📏 💱

Currency & Metric Conversions

Converting metres to centimetres (×100), kilometres to metres (×1000), or checking supermarket prices relies on shifting place-value digits accurately and rounding to the nearest tenth.

💻 🧩

Coding & Patterns

Game programmers use variables (like x and n) and position-to-term rules to calculate player scores, level XP, and repeating tile patterns without listing every single step by hand!

🧠 Reasoning Lab — Linh & Nam's Classic Traps

Debate each statement with your partner using the English sentence stems below, then tap to reveal the mathematical proof.

🔍 Who Is Correct?

Rounding 4.96 to the Nearest Tenth

Linh says: "4.96 rounds to 4.9 because the tenths digit is 9."

Nam says: "4.96 rounds to 5.0 because 6 hundredths rounds 9 tenths up to 10 tenths."

✅ Nam is correct! Since the hundredths digit is 6 (≥ 5), we round 4.9 up by 0.1 to 5.0. Keep the ".0" to show it was rounded to 1 decimal place.
⚖️ Always, Sometimes, Never

"A cube number is an even number."

Test the first five cube numbers (1³ to 5³) on your whiteboard to prove whether this is Always, Sometimes, or Never true.

✅ SOMETIMES TRUE! Even³ = Even (2³ = 8, 4³ = 64), but Odd³ = Odd (1³ = 1, 3³ = 27, 5³ = 125).
💡 Convince Me

Place Value Proof

Convince your partner that 0.7 × 1000 is ten times bigger than 7 × 10, without saying "just add zeros".

✅ 0.7 × 1000 = 700 (digit 7 shifts 3 places left from tenths to hundreds). 7 × 10 = 70 (digit 7 shifts 1 place left to tens). Since 700 = 10 × 70, it is 10 times bigger!

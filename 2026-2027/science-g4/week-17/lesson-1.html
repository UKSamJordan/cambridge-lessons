S • L • G

How are particles arranged in a solid, a liquid and a gas?

👆 Tap to reveal answer

Answer • Unit 3.1 (Links to Today!)

Solid: Packed tightly in fixed rows, vibrating on the spot.

Liquid: Close together, able to slide past each other.

Gas: Far apart, moving freely and quickly in all directions.

Bridge: This explains why sound races through solids!

Real-World Science: Protecting Ears & Soundproofing

How engineers use soft materials and smart designs to keep our hearing safe every day!

🎧 Airport Ear Defenders

Jet engines reach 130 dB. Ground crew wear thick foam-lined ear defenders that trap air and absorb dangerous vibrations before they reach the eardrum.

🚗 Car & Motorbike Silencers

Exhaust pipes contain a silencer (muffler) with acoustic chambers and fibreglass padding that weaken noisy engine vibrations on busy Hanoi streets.

🏫 Classroom Acoustic Panels

Soft fabric wall panels, curtains and carpets absorb bouncing sound waves instead of reflecting echoes, keeping our learning space calm and below 65 dB.

🧭 Steps to Success & Safety

  1. 1 Link to Particles: Closer particles pass vibrations faster (Solid ➔ Liquid ➔ Gas).
  2. 2 Check Units: Always write sound volume in decibels (dB).
  3. 3 Fair Test Rule: Change ONE variable (material); keep source, distance & thickness the same!
  4. 4 Ear Safety: Never shout or bang objects right next to a partner's ear. Keep classroom tests below 85 dB!
💡 Classroom Desk Test (Zero Extra Kit!): Tap your desk gently while listening through air, then press one ear flat onto the wooden desk and tap with the same gentle force. Notice how much louder and clearer it sounds!

Microscopic View: Soft Fibres + Trapped Air Pockets!

1. Hard, smooth surfaces like thin foil reflect and pass vibrations straight through, so the buzzer stays loud (84 dB).

2. Soft, fluffy materials like cotton wool and thick foam are full of tangled fibres and tiny trapped air pockets.

3. Sound vibrations bounce around inside those tiny gaps and lose energy as gentle warmth — so much less vibration reaches our ears (48 dB — SAFE!).

Check 1 • MUST

A pupil taps a wooden desk gently. Does the sound reach her ear faster through the solid wood or through the air? Explain using the word particles.

Through the solid wood! Particles in solid wood are packed tightly together, so they bump into neighbouring particles and pass the vibration along much faster than far-apart air particles.
Check 2 • SHOULD

A music speaker measures 92 dB. After wrapping it in soft fleece, it measures 57 dB. How many decibels quieter did it become, and is it now in the safe zone?

92 − 57 = 35 dB quieter! Yes — 57 dB is safely below 70 dB (well below the 85 dB danger threshold).
Check 3 • COULD

Minh wants to test which cloth muffles a kitchen timer best. Name ONE variable he must change and TWO variables he must keep the same.

Change: The type of cloth.
Keep the same (any 2): Same kitchen timer, same distance to the sound level meter, same cloth thickness / number of layers.

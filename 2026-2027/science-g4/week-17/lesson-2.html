The 3 Biggest Unit 2 Exam Traps

🎯 Exam Clinic Steps

  1. 1 Ask: Is it LOUDER/QUIETER (Volume) or HIGHER/LOWER (Pitch)?
  2. 2 Name what vibrates: string, drum skin, metal bar, or air column.
  3. 3 Link vibration: Bigger = Louder; Faster = Higher pitch.
  4. 4 Check charts: Circle any bar breaking the smooth trend and say "Repeat that trial!"

❌ Common Exam Trap

"Hitting a drum harder makes a higher pitch!"

✅ Golden Cambridge Truth

Hit harder = BIGGER vibrations = LOUDER volume.
Shorter / tighter / thinner = FASTER vibrations = HIGHER pitch!

❌ Common Exam Trap

"More water in the bottle makes a deeper, lower sound when you blow!"

✅ Golden Cambridge Truth

When you BLOW, the AIR vibrates! More water leaves a SHORTER air column ➔ FASTER vibrations ➔ HIGHER pitch!

How Air Particles Carry Volume & Pitch

Sound travels when a vibrating source bumps neighbouring air particles back and forth. Bigger back-and-forth swings pass more energy ➔ louder volume. Faster back-and-forth shakes hit your eardrum more often per second ➔ higher pitch!

1. Must • Volume vs Pitch

Linh taps a xylophone bar much more gently. Does the pitch change, or does the volume change? Write the vibration reason!

✅ Volume becomes quieter because the vibrations are smaller. The pitch stays the same!
2. Should • Air Columns

Tube X has 14 cm of air and Tube Y has 6 cm of air. Which makes the higher pitch when blown, and why?

✅ Tube Y makes the higher pitch because its shorter air column vibrates faster!
3. Could • Anomaly Clinic

Sound meter readings at 1 m, 2 m, 3 m, 4 m, 5 m are: 72 dB, 64 dB, 81 dB, 48 dB, 40 dB. Spot the anomaly and state what to do!

✅ 81 dB at 3 m is anomalous (expected ~56 dB). Repeat the 3 m trial keeping the sound source the same!

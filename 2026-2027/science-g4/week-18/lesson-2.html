Real-World Science • Vets, Zoo Nutritionists & Doctors

Why do real scientists build models of organs we cannot see?

We cannot look inside a living person, dog, or eagle while they digest lunch! Doctors and veterinarians build safe physical and computer models to study how food moves, design healthy diets for zoo animals, and teach students how every organ works together — while always remembering what a model cannot show.

Myth Buster

Myth Buster!

Does the stomach absorb nutrients into the blood?

NO! The stomach churns and mixes food with acid. The small intestine absorbs tiny dissolved nutrients into the blood!

Think Like a Scientist: Strengths & Limitations of Our Model (5TWSm.02)

Explain Why
Strengths (What the Model Shows Well)
  • Shows the correct 1-way order of organs from mouth to anus safely outside the body.
  • Squeezing the bag clearly models stomach muscles churning food into liquid soup.
  • The fine cloth filter shows why only tiny dissolved nutrients pass into the blood while big lumps stay inside.
Limitations (What the Model CANNOT Show)
  • A plastic bag has NO real living stomach acid or digestive juices breaking food down chemically.
  • There are NO real blood vessels carrying absorbed nutrients away to cells around the body.
  • Real digestion takes many hours (up to 24–48 hours), whereas the classroom model takes minutes.

SC 1 • Model Evaluation

In our classroom model, what organ does the fine cloth filter represent — and name ONE limitation of the bag-and-filter model.

Model Answer: Fine cloth filter = Small intestine (absorbs dissolved nutrients). Limitation = No real stomach acid, no living muscles, or no blood vessels carrying nutrients away.
SC 2 • Vertebrate Comparison

Name ONE similarity and ONE difference between a human's digestive system and a bird's digestive system.

Model Answer: Similarity = Both have a 1-way tube with an oesophagus, stomach, and intestines. Difference = Birds have a beak and grit-filled gizzard (no teeth), whereas humans chew with teeth.
SC 3 • Scientific Conclusion

Finish the conclusion: "Breaking food into tiny soluble pieces is essential because..."

Model Answer: "...only tiny dissolved nutrients can pass through the wall of the small intestine and be absorbed into the blood to travel around the body."

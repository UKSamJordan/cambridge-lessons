🌍 Real-World Science: Where Do We See Floating, Sinking & Density?

🚢

Steel Cargo Ships

Solid steel sinks immediately, yet giant cargo ships float! Their hollow hulls trap huge amounts of air, lowering overall density and displacing tonnes of sea water.

🦺

Life Jackets & Foam

Life jackets are filled with light foam packed with tiny air bubbles. This lowers your overall density in water so upthrust keeps your head safely above the surface.

🌊

Submarines

Submarines dive by filling ballast tanks with heavy water (increasing weight & density) and rise back up by pumping air in to push the water out!

🧭 Steps to Success & Safety

  1. 1 Keep the exact same lump of clay for every shape (Fair Test!).
  2. 2 Check if the shape traps air and pushes more water aside.
  3. 3 Draw straight arrows with a ruler: Weight ↓, Upthrust ↑.
  4. 4 Floating still = Equal arrows. Sinking = Weight arrow longer.
⚠️ Lab Safety: Wipe water spills straight away so nobody slips. Keep clay out of sinks!

🔬 Zoom In: Why Does Displaced Water Push Up?

Click to reveal how water particles create upthrust and how trapped air changes overall density.

Water Particles Pushing Up (Upthrust ↑)
Hollow Clay Bowl (Air Inside!)
⬆ ⬆ ⬆ UPTHRUST ⬆ ⬆ ⬆

Water particles are constantly moving and bumping upwards against the hull. Pushing more water out of the way means MORE upward push (greater upthrust)!

Primary Density: Tightly Packed vs Spread Out
Solid Ball (50 g)

Mass tightly packed in tiny space ➔ High density (Sinks)

Hollow Bowl (50 g + Air)
AIR POCKET

Same 50 g + trapped air over big space ➔ Lower overall density (Floats)

Key Idea: An object floats if its overall density (material + trapped air) is less than water's density.

MUST Check • Same Clay Mass

Maya shapes a 40 g lump of clay into a solid cube (it sinks). She reshapes the exact same clay into a deep cup (it floats). What stayed the SAME?

Answer: The MASS (still 40 g) and WEIGHT (still 0.4 N) stayed the same! Only the shape changed.
SHOULD Check • Force Arrows

A toy raft floats still on a pond. Its weight is 2 N pointing downwards. What is the size and direction of the upthrust?

Answer: Upthrust = 2 N pointing straight UP! The two arrows must be EQUAL in length (balanced forces).
COULD Check • Density & Displacement

Explain in one sentence why a heavy hollow canoe floats while a tiny solid pebble sinks.

Answer: The hollow canoe traps air (lower overall density) and displaces enough water for upthrust to balance its weight; the pebble is tightly packed (denser than water).

Red-Flag Misconceptions to Squash
  • "Heavy sinks, light floats": Show the 80 g wooden block floating while the 15 g steel nail sinks!
  • "Reshaping clay makes it lighter": Weigh the clay lump before and after shaping — still 50 g!
  • "Floating means upthrust > weight": Clarify that floating still at the surface means EQUAL (balanced) arrows.
Classroom Clay Lab Tip

Give each pair one 50 g lump of oil-based modelling clay (Plasticine) and a clear plastic tub half-filled with water. Mark the starting water level with a whiteboard pen on the outside of the tub so pupils physically see water rise when displaced!

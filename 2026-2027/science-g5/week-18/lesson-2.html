Real-Life Science in Hanoi

Where do we see Series & Parallel Circuits every day?

🏠 Apartment Ceiling Lights (Parallel)

Every room has its own branch. If the kitchen bulb blows, your bedroom lamp and study fan stay brightly lit!

🔦 Hand Torches (Cells in Series)

Cells sit nose-to-tail (+ to −) inside a torch so voltages add up (1.5 V + 1.5 V = 3.0 V). Put one cell backwards and the torch stays dark!

🎄 Old Festive String Lights (Series)

In an old single-loop string, all bulbs share one pathway. One loose bulb breaks the circuit and turns off the entire row.

Checkpoint Drawing & Lab Rules

  1. 1Use a sharp pencil and ruler for crisp 90° rectangular corners.
  2. 2NEVER place a lamp, switch, buzzer or cell on a corner.
  3. 3Leave a clean empty gap between the tall thin + plate and short thick − plate.
  4. 4In a battery, check polarity: + − joined cleanly to + −.
  5. !Kit Safety: Never wire a cell directly from + to − without a lamp or buzzer (short circuits overheat wires!).
Examiner Tip: If 2 identical lamps are in series, BOTH share the voltage equally — neither bulb is "first in line"!

Data Analysis Clinic (6TWSa.03)

Lamp Brightness in Series vs Parallel Circuits (3.0 V Battery)

How to Read: Check the Y-axis scale (lux), tap any bar to inspect exact brightness, then compare Series vs Parallel!

X: 1 Lamp | Y: 100 lux | Voltage per bulb: 3.0 V

The single lamp receives the full 3.0 V push from the battery, glowing at maximum brightness (100 lux).

2-Mark Checkpoint Conclusion Model

Adding lamps in series shares the 3.0 V push so brightness drops equally (100 ➔ 50 ➔ 33 lux). Adding lamps in parallel gives each branch the full 3.0 V, so every lamp stays equally bright (100 lux).

SC 2 • Brightness & Break

Whiteboard Predict (45s)

Two identical bulbs glow at 100 lux each in a parallel circuit. Bulb A breaks. What happens to Bulb B and why?

Answer: Bulb B stays lit at 100 lux (same brightness) because it has its own complete branch connected directly to the battery!
SC 3 • Fault-Doctor

Whiteboard Diagnose (45s)

A torch has two fresh 1.5 V cells placed with + touching +. The switch is closed, yet the bulb stays dark. Why?

Answer: One cell is reversed! Their pushes oppose each other (1.5 V − 1.5 V = 0 V). Flip one cell so + touches − to get 3.0 V.
